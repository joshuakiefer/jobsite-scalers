# VSL Script: Jobsite Scalers

Target length: 4 to 5 minutes. Record the demo at 1080p full screen with presentation
mode on (press P). Talking-head intro is optional; the demo carries this video. Tone
notes: blunt, plain, zero hype words. You are a guy showing another guy his own money
leaking out of a bucket. Never say "revolutionary", "game changer", or "AI-powered
solution". Say what happens on screen.

Each beat lists WHAT'S ON SCREEN and the VOICEOVER. Keystrokes in [brackets].

---

## Beat 1: The hook (0:00 - 0:25)

**Screen:** Open on the demo website, slowly scrolling the hero.

**VO:**
"This is a plumbing company's website. In the next four minutes I'm going to show you
what happens when someone calls this company and nobody picks up. Because if you run a
service business, that exact thing happened to you this week. Probably today. And it
cost you somewhere between five hundred and five thousand dollars. You just never saw
it happen. This time you're going to see it."

## Beat 2: The site itself (0:25 - 0:55)

**Screen:** Scroll services, job photos, reviews. Hover the Services and Locations
dropdowns. Click Littleton [via nav]. Point at the URL changing.

**VO:**
"First, the boring part that isn't boring. This site has a real page for every service
this company does and every city they work in. Twenty pages. When somebody in Littleton
searches 'plumber near me' at 11pm, one of these pages is what shows up. Your current
site is probably one page your nephew built in 2019. That's not a website, that's a
business card nobody looks at."

[Click Home]

## Beat 3: The money shot (0:55 - 1:50)

**Screen:** [Press 3]. Form fills itself, submits, phone lights up ~5 seconds later.
Let the whole conversation play: customer replies, appointment books for 8am.

**VO:**
"Now watch the right side of the screen. That's the customer's phone. Sarah just filled
out the form... and five seconds later she has a text. Not tomorrow morning. Not 'we'll
get back to you within one business day.' Five seconds. Then watch: she says tomorrow
morning works... and she's booked. 8am, with a tech's name attached. Nobody at the
plumbing company touched anything. The owner was under a sink the whole time.
Here's the stat that should bother you: the business that responds first wins the job
somewhere around 8 out of 10 times. You're not losing jobs because you're bad at
plumbing. You're losing them because you answered second."

## Beat 4: The missed call (1:50 - 2:40)

**Screen:** [Press 1]. Call screen, No Answer, text-back arrives with the "47 seconds"
tag. Conversation plays out to the 2:30 callback.

**VO:**
"Different customer. This one calls, and the call gets missed, because everyone at this
company is working. Normally that's the end of the story. The customer calls the next
plumber on Google. But 47 seconds later this system texts them back. Asks what's going
on. Water heater's leaking. And instead of some robot pretending it can quote a water
heater over text, it does the honest thing: books a time for the lead tech to call them.
2:30 today. That lead was dead. Now it's a callback on the calendar."

## Beat 5: After hours (2:40 - 3:15)

**Screen:** [Press 2]. 11:47 PM status bar, missed call, emergency conversation.

**VO:**
"11:47 at night. Pipe bursts. You're asleep, like a person. The system answers, figures
out it's a real emergency, dispatches, and tells the homeowner to shut off the water
main while help is coming. You wake up to a booked emergency job instead of a voicemail
from someone who already hired your competitor at midnight."

## Beat 6: The review gate (3:15 - 4:05)

**Screen:** [Press 5]. Review request text arrives. Click the link, tap 5 stars, let
the Google review post and slide onto the website, 180 becomes 181. Then [Press 6] to
reset, [Press 5] again, tap 2 stars, let the private path play and the Owner Alert
slide in.

**VO:**
"Job's done. Customer gets one text with one link. If they had a great experience,
they tap five stars and land straight on Google. Watch the website... there's the
review, count just went from 180 to 181. That's the machine feeding itself.
But here's the part nobody shows you. Run it again, and this time the customer's
annoyed. Two stars. They vent, they feel heard, the owner gets their full complaint on
his phone with their number... and Google never sees it. One bad review costs you real
jobs for years. This catches it before it exists."

## Beat 7: The owner's pocket (4:05 - 4:35)

**Screen:** [Press V]. Owner app with the unread badge. Tap Sarah's conversation:
form data card, AI-tagged replies. Tap Bookings. Tap Reviews.

**VO:**
"And this is your side of it. One app. Every lead, every conversation, every booking,
every review. That form Sarah filled out? Name, number, address, problem, sitting in
the thread. The AI handled all of it, and if you ever want to jump in, you just type.
This is not another CRM you have to learn. It's your business, handled, in your pocket."

## Beat 8: The offer and CTA (4:35 - 5:00)

**Screen:** Point at the stats card. Missed Calls Rescued, Jobs Booked, Revenue Saved.

**VO:**
"Everything you just watched is one system: the website that ranks, the five-second
text-back, the after-hours answer, the review engine. We build it for your company,
your trade, your cities, your photos, and it runs whether you're on a roof or asleep.
If you want to see it running with your company's name on it, book a 15 minute call
below. I'll build your demo before we even talk. If you watch it and don't want it,
no hard feelings. But do the math on one missed water heater job a month first."

**End card:** Logo, calendar link, one line: "One missed call pays for the whole year."

---

## Recording checklist

- Click the page once before recording so message sounds play
- P for presentation mode, V flips are part of the show
- Reset (6) between beats 3/4/5 so threads start clean
- Beat 6 requires the reset mid-beat (6, then 5 again) for the two-star run
- Record each beat as its own take, cut together after; the demo resets cleanly
